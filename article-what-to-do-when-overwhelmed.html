What to do when you feel overwhelmed

When everything feels urgent, nothing gets done. Here is how to make the pile smaller.

Too many messages, too many tasks, too many things you should have done yesterday. Overwhelm is not only having a lot to do. It is the feeling that it is all happening at once, and you cannot see where to start. So you scroll, or freeze, or try to do everything badly.

Why your mind freezes

Your mind can only hold a few things at a time. When you try to keep every task, worry and deadline in your head, it treats them all as equally urgent. The way out is to move them out of your head, where you can see them clearly and choose.

A ten-minute reset on paper

  1. Empty your head (3 minutes). Write down everything that is pulling at you, big and small. Do not sort yet. Our brain dump tool works for this too.
  2. Cross out what is not yours (2 minutes). Which things can you not control, or do not need to touch this week? Mark them and set them aside.
  3. Circle three (2 minutes). From what is left, circle no more than three things that matter most today.
  4. Make the first one tiny (3 minutes). Turn the first task into a step you could start in five minutes. Not “do the report”, but “open the file and write the heading”.

Take care of the person carrying the list

The American Psychological Association suggests a few things that help when stress builds. Notice your own warning signs. Remove or reduce a stressor where you can. When you catch yourself imagining the worst, stop and put your attention somewhere else. Keep up exercise and sleep, and reach out to people you trust.

Be kind to yourself, too. Overwhelm is information about your load, not proof that you are failing.

You do not need to see the whole staircase. You only need the next step.

When to get help

If you feel overwhelmed most days for weeks, or it affects your sleep, eating or relationships, talk to a doctor or a mental health professional. You do not have to carry it alone.
